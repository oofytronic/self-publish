1
Set up your KDP account
Before anything else — this takes 10 minutes and you'll need it to save progress.
1
Create or log into KDP at kdp.amazon.com

Sign in with an existing Amazon account or create one. Complete your tax interview and payment/banking info before doing anything else — KDP requires this before publishing. It asks for your Social Security Number (or EIN if you have a publishing imprint) for tax purposes. Takes about 10 minutes.

ⓘ If she already has an Amazon account from her first book, she can use the same KDP login — all previous titles will be there.
2
Format the manuscript
This is done twice — once for the paperback interior, once for Kindle. They're different formats.
1
Choose your trim size first

The most common trim sizes for fiction and narrative nonfiction are 5×8", 5.5×8.5", and 6×9". Pick this before formatting — it determines the page size and margins in your Word file, and you don't want to redo it. When in doubt, 5.5×8.5" is a versatile, clean choice for most books.

2
Format the paperback interior (Word or PDF)

KDP accepts a Word .docx or PDF for the interior. Set the page size in Word to match your trim size (File → Page Setup). Set margins to at least 0.5" on all sides — the gutter (spine side) needs a bit more, around 0.75". Use consistent Heading styles for chapter titles so the table of contents works. Remove headers/footers from the first page of each chapter.

✦ Free shortcut: Download KDP's pre-formatted Word template directly from your KDP dashboard (look under "Paperback resources"). Start by pasting the manuscript into that template instead of formatting from scratch.
3
Format the Kindle eBook interior (separate step)

Kindle uses a reflowable format — the reader adjusts font size, so fixed page layouts don't apply. The simplest path: upload a clean Word .docx with proper Heading 1 / Heading 2 styles applied to chapter titles. KDP will convert it automatically into the Kindle format.

✦ For more control: Download the free Kindle Create app from Amazon. It lets you style and preview the book exactly as it'll look on a Kindle device before uploading.
3
Prepare the cover ⚠ Cover size issue — read this
The most common stumbling block. There are actually three cover files needed.
1
Check and resize the existing front cover art

KDP requires the cover image to be at least 1000px on the shortest side and 2500px on the longest, with a 1.6:1 height-to-width ratio — the ideal target is 1600×2560px. It must be 300 DPI for quality print output. Upload as JPG or TIFF.

! Upscaling a low-resolution image to 300 DPI will not fix the quality — it just makes the pixels bigger. Check the source file's actual resolution first. If the original art was created at 72 DPI, it may need to be recreated or professionally upscaled.
✦ Free fix: Upload the image to Canva (free). Go to Resize, enter 1600×2560px, and download as PDF or PNG. Check that it still looks sharp at that size — if it looks blurry, the source resolution is too low.
2
Build the full paperback wrap cover (front + spine + back)

The paperback needs a full wrap-around cover — front cover, spine, and back cover as a single flat file. The spine width is calculated based on your page count and paper type. Use KDP's free Cover Calculator tool (in the KDP dashboard) to get the exact spine width, then download the blank template PDF. Design the full wrap around that template in Canva, Photoshop, or Affinity Publisher. Export as a PDF with bleed included.

ⓘ In Canva, search "KDP book cover" and enter your exact dimensions from the Cover Calculator. Canva's free plan handles this fine. Make sure the spine text (title + author name) is legible — it's easy to make it too small.
3
Kindle cover (front image only — simpler)

For the Kindle listing, you only need the front cover image — no spine or back. The same resized front cover file from Step 1 works perfectly here. Upload as a JPG.

4
Upload and fill in book details
You'll do this twice — once for the paperback, once for the Kindle eBook.
1
Start a new paperback title in KDP

In your KDP dashboard, click "Create" → "Paperback." Fill in: title, subtitle (if any), series name and number (if applicable), author name, and description. The description is your book's sales pitch on Amazon — write it like back-cover copy. Hook in the first sentence. You also choose up to 7 keywords and 2 BISAC categories (the genre classifications).

✦ Keywords matter for discoverability on Amazon. Think about what a reader would type when searching for a book like yours — specific phrases work better than single words.
2
Decide on the ISBN

KDP offers a free ISBN, but it will list "Independently published" as the publisher name. If she wants a custom publisher name — even just a personal imprint name like "Sage River Press" — she'll need to buy her own ISBN from Bowker at myidentifiers.com. Pricing: $125 for one, $295 for 10.

ⓘ For most authors, the free KDP ISBN is completely fine. The publisher name is visible on the Amazon page, but most readers don't notice or care. Buying your own ISBN matters more if you're selling through libraries or bookstores.
3
Upload the files and set pricing

Upload the interior PDF and the full-wrap cover PDF. KDP will show a live previewer — check it page by page. Then set your list price. KDP shows the minimum price based on printing cost (varies by page count, paper type, and trim size). For royalties: paperback earns 60% minus the printing cost per copy; Kindle eBooks priced $2.99–$9.99 earn 70%.

4
Repeat for the Kindle eBook

Go back to "Create" → "Kindle eBook" and repeat the process with the eBook interior file and front cover image. After both are created, link them together — KDP will ask if you want to link the paperback and eBook on the same product page so they share reviews.

✦ Always link the paperback and Kindle versions. Shared reviews build social proof faster on a single listing than split across two.
5
Review and publish
The final checks before going live.
1
Order a physical proof copy — do not skip this

Before publishing publicly, order an author proof copy. KDP charges only the printing cost (typically $3–7 depending on page count) and ships it to you. Hold the physical book and check: margins on every page, fonts rendering correctly, chapter headers, cover color accuracy, back cover text, and whether the spine text is legible.

! Cover colors almost always print differently than they look on screen. Bright blues tend to go darker; certain reds shift. Check the proof before approving — it's much easier to fix now than after hundreds of copies have sold.
2
Approve and publish

Once happy with everything, click "Publish." The paperback will be live on Amazon within 72 hours; the Kindle eBook within 24–48 hours. Both will appear on the same Amazon product page if linked. She'll receive an email confirmation when each goes live.

1
Set up a Lulu account
Quick signup — no payment info needed upfront.
1
Create a free account at lulu.com

Go to lulu.com and sign up. Lulu is free to publish on — you only pay for physical proof copies or if you want premium distribution features. Add payment info when prompted during the setup wizard.

2
Format the manuscript
Similar to KDP, but Lulu provides its own templates — and the eBook path is a bit different.
1
Download Lulu's free Word template for your trim size

Lulu has pre-formatted Word templates for every trim size at lulu.com/create/templates. Download the one matching your chosen size and paste the manuscript into it. Adjust fonts, heading styles, and spacing to taste.

2
Export to PDF with embedded fonts

In Word, go to File → Save As → PDF. Before saving, open "Options" and check "ISO 19005-1 compliant (PDF/A)" or simply ensure "Embed fonts in the file" is checked. This is critical — if fonts aren't embedded, Lulu will either reject the file or the text will render incorrectly.

! This is the single most common upload error on Lulu. Always double-check that fonts are embedded before uploading the PDF.
3
For the Kindle eBook — use KDP directly instead

Lulu does offer eBook distribution, but it routes through a third-party aggregator and the royalty cut is noticeably lower than publishing directly on KDP. The popular approach: use Lulu for the paperback, and publish the Kindle version directly on KDP. This is fully allowed as long as she doesn't enroll the Kindle version in KDP Select (which requires exclusivity).

✦ Recommended hybrid: Lulu for print + KDP direct for Kindle. Best of both worlds — Lulu's easier print workflow and KDP's higher eBook royalties.
3
Prepare the cover ⚠ Cover size issue — read this
Lulu's built-in cover builder makes this significantly easier than doing it manually.
1
Use Lulu's built-in cover builder (recommended)

Inside the Lulu project wizard, there's a drag-and-drop cover builder. Upload the front cover art as an image and Lulu automatically calculates the spine width and lays out the full wrap. This is the easiest path — no Photoshop or Canva required.

✦ This is the biggest advantage Lulu has over KDP — you don't need to manually calculate the spine width or build a full wrap cover from scratch. Lulu handles all of that once you upload the front art.
! The front cover art still needs to be at least 300 DPI. If the current file is a low-resolution JPG (from a web save, for example), it will look blurry in print. Check the source file's actual resolution before uploading.
2
Or upload a custom full-wrap cover PDF (advanced)

If she wants full design control, Lulu has a Cover Template Generator in the project dashboard that outputs the exact dimensions for the full wrap. Design around that template in Canva or Photoshop and upload as a PDF with crop marks and bleed included.

4
Set up the project and book details
Lulu's guided wizard walks through each field — nothing is hidden.
1
Start a new print book project

Click "Create" → "Print Book." The wizard will ask: trim size, paper type (white or cream — cream is traditional for fiction), binding type (perfect bound for paperback), and whether the interior is black & white or color. Choose based on the manuscript's content.

2
Fill in title, author, and metadata

Title, author name, book description, genre category. Lulu assigns a free ISBN automatically for distributed books. If she has her own ISBN from Bowker, she can enter it here instead.

3
Choose distribution

Lulu's "Global Distribution" option (free to enable) sends the book to Amazon, Barnes & Noble, and other retail channels. However, it takes 6–8 weeks to appear on Amazon this way — much slower than KDP's 72-hour listing. If speed matters, consider publishing the paperback on KDP directly and using Lulu as a separate channel for direct/personal sales.

ⓘ Running Lulu and KDP simultaneously for the same paperback is allowed. Just make sure the prices are consistent — Amazon may suppress Lulu's listing if KDP shows a lower price.
4
Set pricing

Lulu shows the base printing cost per copy. Set your retail price above that — the difference is your revenue minus Lulu's platform fee. Royalties through Lulu's distribution are generally lower than KDP direct, but the difference matters less for print than for eBooks.

5
Review and publish
Lulu's previewer is excellent — use it before ordering a proof.
1
Preview in Lulu's online 3D previewer

Lulu renders a 3D preview of the book before any printing happens. Flip through every page in the viewer — check margins, chapter page breaks, fonts, cover wrap alignment, and back cover text. Fix any issues and re-upload before ordering a proof.

2
Order a physical proof copy

Order one copy at printing cost. Lulu ships it to you directly. Check everything in hand — cover finish (matte vs glossy), paper weight, interior contrast, spine text legibility, and margins on the outermost pages.

! Don't skip the physical proof. Screens show color differently than ink on paper — and small layout issues (text too close to the edge) only become obvious when you're physically turning pages.
3
Approve and enable distribution

Once the proof looks good, click "Approve" in the Lulu dashboard and enable distribution. The Lulu storefront listing is immediate. Amazon and other retailers via Global Distribution take 6–8 weeks. She'll get email updates as each channel goes live.

The recommended hybrid approach

Many self-published authors do exactly this: use Lulu for the paperback (easier cover builder, nice direct storefront for selling personally) and publish the Kindle eBook directly on KDP (70% royalty, goes live in 24 hours). You get the best of both tools without handing anything off to a third party. This is fully permitted — as long as the Kindle version isn't enrolled in KDP Select, there's no exclusivity conflict.
